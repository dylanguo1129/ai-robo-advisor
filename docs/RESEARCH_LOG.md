## 2. Back-test design, as first run (26 Sep 2026, midday)

Walk-forward, 60-month estimation window, annual re-estimation, weights held 12 months. Five methods x three profiles. No transaction costs. Each method used its own lam.

**Post-hoc change, disclosed.** After seeing the first table it was clear that "high" under sample-mean mu had 10% realized volatility while "high" under equilibrium mu had 4%: the same lam produces different risk when mu is on a different scale, so the profiles were not comparable across methods. The back-test was changed to *risk-matched*: every method's lam is calibrated so its ex-ante volatility hits 12 / 9 / 4% per profile. This is a change to the comparison, not to the method being sold, but under the protocol ("iterated out of sample is not out of sample") it must be recorded: the reported results are from the second design, and the first design's numbers are kept in git history of this folder (`backtest_stats.csv` as of the midday run: sample-mean high profile 16.6% return at 10.0% vol).

## 3. Changes after reading the Session 2 materials (26 Sep 2026, afternoon)

All added before the robustness grid was run:

* transaction costs, 10 bps one-way on every unit of turnover including monthly drift rebalancing ("Do not ignore trading costs and fees");
* stationary block bootstrap (mean block 6 months) replaces the iid bootstrap as the default resampling, to keep volatility clustering;
* `integrity_report`: stale-price runs and |monthly return| > 30% are flagged, never deleted ("Do not arbitrarily exclude outliers");
* `cash_max`, `naive_blend` (Tu & Zhou 2011) and a policy-prior helper, so the grid below can test them;
* sub-period statistics.

## 5. What was *not* tried, and why

* No hyper-parameter search over delta, the profile multipliers, the shrinkage target, the cap or the window length. Each has one pre-specified value or a single pre-specified alternative in the grid.
* No winsorization or outlier removal at any level.
* No learned allocator (decision-focused learning, neural shrinkage) — see METHOD.md section 4.
* No look-ahead: AUM is a single 2026 snapshot used as the prior at every date, which is a *known* mild look-ahead (the 2026 AUM ranking reflects 2016-2026 performance). Recorded as a limitation; a time-varying prior needs AUM history the team does not have.

## 6. v2 pre-registration (written 28 Sep 2026, before any v2 back-test was run)

Scope change: the universe is now every CAD-buyable Canadian-listed ETF and every US-listed ETF; the portfolio is built on a reference shelf (one broad, low-cost fund per asset class, chosen by a written rule in `robokit/shelf.py`) plus optional user picks. Young shelf funds are extended with written, correlation-checked proxies (`robokit/history.py`). The risk level (1-5) sets a share of the stock market's volatility (level x 20%), and lambda is calibrated to it.

**What will be run (`scripts/run_backtest_v2.py`), exactly once:**

* Markets: CA (CAD, reference shelf) and US (USD, reference shelf), spliced histories.
* Walk-forward: 60-month rolling estimation window, re-estimated every 12 months, held 12 months with monthly rebalancing back to target; 10 bps one-way cost on all turnover (v1 protocol).
* Levels 2, 3, 4 (the brief's low / mid / high).
* Strategies:
  1. `robokit`: equilibrium returns, shrunk covariance, guardrails of `robokit/engine.py` (sleeve caps, cash cap by level), stationary-bootstrap resampling with 50 draws, lambda calibrated so the in-window volatility equals level share x in-window stock-market volatility.
  2. `robokit_trend`: the same plus Black-Litterman views on every non-cash shelf fund, view = equilibrium excess return + sigma_i x IC x z_i with z_i = 12-1 month return / annual volatility, clipped to +/-2, IC = 0.05 (Grinold's "good forecaster"), confidence 50%, tilt bounded to 1% tracking error and 5 points per fund.
  3. `static_mix`: equity share = level share (40 / 60 / 80%), equity split by the policy prior's equity proportions and the rest by its fixed-income proportions, rebalanced monthly. This is the classic balanced-fund benchmark.
  4. `sample_mvo`: the plug-in (sample-mean) mean-variance with the same covariance, caps and calibration: the textbook foil.
  5. `equal_weight`: 1/N over the non-cash shelf funds (not risk-matched; reported for reference).
* Reported: annual return, volatility, Sharpe versus the cash sleeve, maximum drawdown, turnover, cost drag, realised versus target volatility, sub-periods 2020 and 2022, and paired t-statistics of monthly return differences (robokit vs each other strategy; robokit_trend vs robokit).

**Decision rules fixed now:**

* The trend tilt becomes an opt-in product feature only if `robokit_trend` beats `robokit` at level 3 in BOTH markets with a paired t-statistic above 2. Otherwise the tilt stays off and the product says it was tested and did not pass.
* Robo-Kit's default is not changed by any v2 result. If `robokit` loses to `static_mix` or `sample_mvo`, that is reported as found.
* Known biases, stated in advance: (a) the shelf is chosen with 2026 information (today's cheapest, largest funds), a mild selection bias; (b) proxies are real funds that existed at the time, but choosing them uses 2026 knowledge of which funds survived; (c) no delisted ETFs are in the data (survivorship), which matters little for broad index funds but is not zero.

**v2 results (run 28 Sep 2026, once, 82 s for CA and 136 s for US).** Level 3, Robo-Kit minus other strategy, annualised, after costs (t-statistic): Canada (Dec 2009 to Aug 2026): vs static mix -0.2% (-1.1), vs textbook +0.1% (0.1), vs equal weights +0.8% (1.3); trend minus base +0.2% (1.3). US (Jul 2018 to Aug 2026): vs static -0.2% (-0.7), vs textbook -1.5% (-2.1), vs equal +1.2% (1.1); trend minus base +0.5% (2.6). Decision rule: the trend tilt needed t > 2 in both markets at level 3; it passed in the US and failed in Canada, so it stays off. Realised volatility at level 3: 6.9% vs 6.8% target in Canada, 10.3% vs 8.7% in the US. Nothing was changed after these results.

**Correction after the independent code review (28 Sep 2026, same day).** The review found that `robokit/backtest.py` charged the 10 bps cost only on the monthly drift back to target, not on the move from one year's target to the next, although the protocol above says "cost on all turnover" (v1 charged both). The same review found gold and broad-commodity funds classed as equity, which changed the US gold sleeve's reference fund from IAUM to GLDM (same exposure, fee 0.10% vs 0.09%). Both were fixed and the back-test was re-run once with nothing else changed. The first run is kept in `output/v2/backtest_run1_before_review/`. Effect: every return moved by 0.03 points a year or less; turnover now counts the annual move (level 3 textbook optimizer: 2.6% a month instead of 0.8%). Level 3 results after the correction: Canada vs static -0.2% (t -1.1), vs textbook +0.1% (0.2), vs equal +0.8% (1.3), trend minus base +0.2% (1.3); US vs static -0.2% (-0.7), vs textbook -1.5% (-2.1), vs equal +1.2% (1.1), trend minus base +0.5% (2.5). No conclusion changed, and the trend tilt stays off.

## 7. Self-check scoring and projection window, v2.1 (written 28 Sep 2026, before the changes were run)

Why: seven persona walkthroughs (docs/qa/ux/) found that the capacity cap binds far too often and that the "bad year" figure never saw 2008. Changes, decided before looking at any new output:

1. Capacity deductions for share of savings (B4), uneven income (B5) and a partial emergency fund (A2) combine to at most 2 steps, and at most 1 step when the horizon score is 13 or more. Before: up to 3 separate steps (a 20+ year saver with a partial emergency fund could land on level 2).
2. B2 (how long withdrawals last) gets a "Not sure yet" option worth 3 points, the middle of its scale. When the goal is "building wealth, no fixed date" (A1 = wealth), B2 is not asked and scores 3.
3. Gate G1 ("needs the money within 2 years") fires only when the money is also taken out all at once (B1 = within 2 years and B2 = all at once). A phased drawdown is scored normally. B1's first option is relabelled "In less than 2 years".
4. The suggestion stays the lower of capacity and tolerance. It stops being a ceiling: a person may choose a higher level after a page sheet shows that level's bad year and crisis replays in their own dollars, and the choice is recorded. Gates G2 and G3 (no emergency fund, a lot of high-interest debt) still cap the build at level 2.
5. Projections (one-year range, bad year, ten-year paths, holding-period ranges, the range cards in the self-check) resample from the longest history where every fund in the mix has data, spliced and back-filled as disclosed, instead of the 15-year estimation window. For the reference funds that history starts in the mid-2000s and includes 2008. The weights are still estimated on the 15-year window. Satellites are back-filled over the same long history so crisis replays stop disappearing.

Not changed: the pre-registered back-test (section 6), the optimizer, the level-to-volatility mapping.

## 9. v3 engine changes (written 8 Oct 2026, before any v3 evaluation was run)

Source: the v3 design spec (docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-08-optimizer-v3-design.md), built from a reproduced code audit, published industry methodology, verified literature, and reviews by two outside examiners from different model families (their findings and the rulings on them are in docs/DECISIONS.md). None of these changes was chosen by looking at a back-test.

1. Safety (no effect on the reference mix): one request validator in both engines; limits that cannot all hold are refused by default, or relaxed in a fixed, recorded order on request; the 2% minimum is kept after a view tilt; solvers fail closed with a convergence certificate; no hard-coded stock-market volatility; level labels round halves up within 1-5; a fund swap must keep the slot's asset class and region and cannot bring in a satellite-only fund.
2. Data integrity: 14 funds whose monthly return doubled or halved while similar funds did not are no longer eligible (7 Canadian, 7 US). No reference fund is affected.
3. One random generator in both engines (mulberry32, the browser's), for resampling and projections; QIS in the browser; the exact capped-simplex projection and the exact largest eigenvalue for the step size in both engines. Python and the browser agree to rounding on every parity case.
4. Lambda is calibrated on the final pipeline (resampled, minimum position applied, the same draws at every step): bracket, then Illinois regula falsi on log lambda (Dowell and Jarratt 1971) with a bisection safeguard. Each result is within 0.005 percentage points of volatility of the target (5e-5 as a decimal; the constant CAL_TOL), a proven jump of the minimum-position rule, or a bound of lambda; a result that stops at the 30-evaluation limit is recorded as a failed calibration. A jump or out-of-reach result carries its evidence (the last bracket: lambda and volatility at each end; or the bound of lambda and the volatility there), which the tests re-check by rebuilding at the lambdas it names. Reasons: resampled averages drift off the risk target (Scherer 2002; level 5 ended 0.23 points below target in v2), and the volatility of averaged resampled weights is not monotone in lambda (tests/test_v3_propositions.py), so a bisection that assumes it could stop at the wrong place.
5. Implementation layer: a rebalancer (cash flows first; bands of min(2 points, 25% of target) with a destination at 7/8 of the band; whole units; minimum order; residual cash; cost and a liquidity flag); the NI 81-102 Appendix F rating of every mix; opt-in suitability checks (off by default; the level policy is unchanged).

## 10. v3 evaluation pre-registration (written 8 Oct 2026, revised after fifteen Stage 1 reviews the same day, before any v3 evaluation was run)

The examiners' fifteen Stage 1 reviews of this plan and its code, and the ruling on each point, are in docs/DECISIONS.md. Every required change they made is adopted below unless the ruling says why not.

**Protocol.** One script, `scripts/run_v3_evaluation.py`, with its library (`robokit/evaluation.py`, `robokit/simulation.py`, `robokit/audit.py`, `robokit/evalstats.py`) and tests (`tests/test_evalstats.py`, `tests/test_v3_research.py`, `tests/test_v3_propositions.py`, `tests/test_v3_fixtures.py`), is committed before the run. The run refuses to start from a working tree with uncommitted changes (including new, untracked files under scripts/, robokit/, optimizer/ and tests/) and records the commit (it refuses without a git checkout and a full commit id), the data snapshot id, the package versions and the number of simulated histories (it refuses anything but 500) in `output/v3/results.json`, which holds every number the paper uses (an infinite value, such as the minimum track record when robokit is not ahead, is written as "inf"). It runs once; a re-run is allowed only for a disclosed bug fix with nothing else changed, the first run archived (section 6 rule). Before the run the harness was checked only by a two-date dry run and smoke runs of each stage on tiny inputs (two or three dates, two simulated histories); they printed counts and the names of the keys produced, never a number, and their outputs were deleted. Two smoke runs also printed which reading labels the two-date US comparison produced, to check that the reading code runs; on two dates those labels carry no information about the result. An unsupported research switch makes the engine refuse. A strategy's path stops, and is recorded under `path_errors` with its stage, market, strategy and level, when a held fund has no return in a holding month (a missing return is never treated as a flat month), when the return months are not consecutive, or when a scheduled estimation date has no build (the previous weights would otherwise be held past their 12 months); E2's registered skip of leading dates is the one exception (below). An excess return needs the cash slot's return for that month: a missing one is recorded as an error, never treated as zero. Fund returns come from adjusted closes, which are net of each fund's fee; no further fee is deducted; trading costs are 10 bps one way on all turnover.

**Order.** E3 runs first. Its two decision rules (resampling, the QIS switch) lock the defaults in `results.json` under `locked_defaults`; every later part runs on the locked configuration and no later result can change a default. E4 is a back-test, so it is descriptive only (section 6 rule: no back-test result changes a default). After the run, the product's constants are set to the locked defaults in both engines (the QIS switch, resampling, and the projection path count), in one disclosed commit; tests/test_v3_locked_defaults.py fails whenever a shipped constant differs from the locked defaults in results.json.

**Power, stated in advance.** Out-of-sample months under the expanding-window rule: about 200 in Canada (January 2010 to August 2026) and about 92 in the US (January 2019 to August 2026). For two risk-matched balanced strategies with monthly return correlation near 0.9, the standard error of an annual Sharpe-ratio difference is roughly sqrt(12 x 2 (1 - 0.9) / T): about 0.11 in Canada and 0.16 in the US, so at the 5% level a difference must be about 0.22 (Canada) or 0.31 (US) to be detected half the time, and about 0.31 (Canada) or 0.45 (US) to be detected 80% of the time (1.96 and 2.80 standard errors). The Sharpe comparisons are therefore expected to be inconclusive unless a difference is large, and are read that way: the US comparisons are exploratory.

**E1. Walk-forward of the shipped configuration (fixed shelf).** CA and US reference shelves, extended back with their written stand-ins (`engine.as_of(date, "fixed")`: the returns, interest rates and exchange rates used for estimation stop at the date). Disclosed look-ahead: which funds, which stand-ins, and whether a stand-in passes its 24-month, 0.8-correlation check all use the data snapshot (today's knowledge); E2 removes it. Estimation dates: every December month-end from the first with 60 months of common history to the last before the data end; window expanding, capped at 180 months; weights held 12 months and rebalanced monthly to target, 10 bps on the monthly drift and on the annual change. Levels 2, 3 and 4 primary; levels 1 and 5 for realised versus target volatility only (each with its own 90% interval, labelled descriptive and without the reading's labels).

Strategies. `robokit` (the shipped engine: 200 resampled histories, seed 0, final-pipeline calibration). Confirmatory benchmarks, all aimed at the same target: `static_mix` (stock share 40/60/80%, split by the policy prior among the equity slots, the rest among the bond slots, no real estate, gold or cash, as v2; matched to the target by construction, since level L targets L x 20% of the stock market's volatility, not calibrated: its ex-ante gap to the target is reported with every strategy's); `textbook_60` (sample means, the same shrunk covariance and caps, single-solve calibration, 60-month rolling window, no resampling, no minimum position: the v2 textbook optimizer); `erc_cash` (equal risk contribution across the non-cash funds, mixed with cash to the target; the solve fails closed unless the risk contributions are equal within a relative 1e-6, and a failed build stops its path; above its own volatility it is used as is and counted as unreachable); `minvar_cash` (long-only minimum variance across non-cash funds, 50% cap, mixed with cash to the target). Descriptive: `equal_weight` (not risk-matched) and the ablations, each one component changed through the engine's research switches: `no_resample`, `sample_cov`, `no_caps` (sleeve and cash caps removed, the 50% per-fund maximum kept), `no_cash_caps`, `single_lambda` (v2 calibration), `sample_mu`, `james_stein_mu` (Jorion's Bayes-Stein means).

Returns and Sharpe. Monthly net returns; excess returns over the cash slot's monthly return (its extended series, the bill before the fund existed); Sharpe = mean / standard deviation of monthly excess returns, times sqrt(12), the same formula for reporting and testing. Metrics: annual (geometric) return, volatility, Sharpe, maximum drawdown, full-sample monthly CVaR(95%) with the number of tail months, turnover, cost drag; 2020 and 2022 return, volatility and drawdown (no tail measure on a single year); the ex-ante gap between each mix's model volatility and its target.

Risk delivery. For each level, the monthly returns divided by the monthly target then in force (target / sqrt 12); the ratio is their standard deviation (1 when the mix delivers its target). Simultaneous 90% intervals over levels 2-4 (Bonferroni: 96.7% each, circular block bootstrap, block 6, 5,000 draws). Reading fixed now: "delivered" only if every interval lies inside [0.80, 1.25]; "missed at level L" if the point ratio at L lies outside that band; otherwise "inconclusive". (A simulation check of the interval, computed by the run and written to results.json: 1,000 samples of 120 i.i.d. months, 999 bootstrap draws each, block 6, at the nominal 90% of one interval, the 96.7% of each of three simultaneous ones, and the joint coverage of three such intervals on series correlated 0.9 (as the levels' paths are), which is what "delivered" relies on, and how often all three intervals lie inside [0.80, 1.25] when the true ratio is 1 (how often a mix that delivers is read as delivered). The test suite's smaller version finds about 87% for a nominal 90%: the interval runs slightly short, which makes "delivered" slightly easier to reach; reported with the results.)

Inference. Confirmatory family: `robokit` against the four confirmatory benchmarks at levels 2, 3 and 4 (12 comparisons per market). For each: the Sharpe difference by the Ledoit-Wolf (2008) studentized circular block bootstrap (block 6, 5,000 draws, one seed so the blocks are common to all comparisons; blocks 3 and 12 as sensitivity), its studentized 95% interval, the two-sided p-value, and a one-sided non-inferiority p-value for H0: robokit's annual Sharpe is lower by 0.10 or more; Holm adjustment of each set of 12 p-values; a comparison without a path enters its set with p = 1, so the family is always the registered 12 and a missing path never makes another comparison easier to pass. Reading fixed now, and written into `results.json` by the code (robokit/evaluation.py `reading`, tested branch by branch): with a Holm-adjusted two-sided p below 0.05, "benchmark better" or "robokit better" by the sign of the difference; otherwise "robokit not worse by more than 0.10" with a Holm-adjusted non-inferiority p below 0.05; otherwise "too short to tell" when robokit's Sharpe is ahead (the code checks the sign of the difference) but the sample has fewer months than the minimum track-record length (Bailey and Lopez de Prado 2012) for it to exceed the benchmark's (when robokit is not ahead that length is infinite, and more of the same data cannot help); otherwise "inconclusive". The minimum track-record length is shown beside every comparison. If a path is missing, or a month has no cash return, the comparisons that need it record the error and enter their Holm family with p = 1; every other registered output (ratings, risk delivery, deflated Sharpe ratios, calibration counts) is computed from the paths that exist, and each output that needs a missing path says so. Mean return differences get Newey-West t-statistics (6 lags), descriptive. Comparisons with `equal_weight` and the ablations are descriptive (no Holm, no reading). The deflated Sharpe ratio (Bailey and Lopez de Prado 2014) for robokit and for the confirmatory benchmark with the highest Sharpe among those with a path, at each level and market, with N = 54 trials (written into the results with their inventory; computed on monthly Sharpe ratios, labelled so in the results; the deflated Sharpe ratio is a probability): section 2 (5 methods), section 4 (7 configurations x 5 methods = 35), section 6 (5 strategies), the 5 E1 confirmatory strategies, and the 4 E3 contrasts that could move a default (resampling; the three QIS tests); the variance of the trial Sharpe ratios from the E1 strategies at that level and market (written beside each deflated Sharpe ratio). The earlier trials cannot be recomputed on the E1 months, so this spread may understate theirs, which makes the deflated Sharpe ratio, if anything, too favourable; the same ratio with N = the E1 strategies only is reported beside it. Both are descriptive, outside the confirmatory reading. Every US comparison row in E1 and E2 (confirmatory, descriptive or an error) is written with the family "exploratory", every Canadian one "confirmatory".

The NI 81-102 Appendix F rating of each level's realised path (its last 120 months) is reported beside the risk delivery, with the number of months it uses; a path shorter than 120 months (the US) is labelled a short-history estimate on the Appendix F scale, not an Appendix F rating (`appendix_f` false).

Calibration (section 9 item 4, decided on principle): robokit against `single_lambda` on the ex-ante gap to target and on the realised ratio; it stays either way, and a worse realised ratio is reported as a limitation. The count of calibration outcomes (within tolerance, jump, out of reach, limit) is reported; any `limit` is a failure and is listed by strategy, date and level (`calibration_failures`). The guarantee is tested before the run: 40 random single-solve requests and five resampled ones end within tolerance, at a proven jump or at a bound, each jump and bound re-checked by rebuilding at the lambdas its evidence names (tests/test_v3_calibration.py).

**E2. Past returns with today's universe (strict).** As E1 for `robokit`, `static_mix` and `textbook_60` at levels 2-4, built with `engine.as_of(date, "strict")`: every series stops at the date before anything is computed; each slot holds the best-scoring fund that already had 36 months of its own returns; no stand-ins (`robokit/research.py pit_shelf`). A slot with no such fund is left out and its policy share spread over the others; a date whose shelf has under 36 months of common own history is skipped and listed (`leading_skips`; only before the first date that builds: a later failure stops the path, as in E1; every build failure is also counted under `build_failures`). The gate itself is tested at its edge: the Canadian reference funds share 35 months of their own history at the end of October 2024 (refused) and 36 a month later (built), tests/test_v3_research.py. This removes the look-ahead in the returns and stand-ins. It does not make the shelf point-in-time: the fund list, its eligibility and the ranking score's inputs (size, trading, fee) are today's, and closed funds are absent; no conclusion about how funds are chosen is drawn from E2. The test `tests/test_v3_research.py` shows the strict mode does not see the future: replacing every later return, rate and exchange rate with noise leaves the mix unchanged. Descriptive: the same metrics (with the 2020 and 2022 subperiods), risk delivery and comparisons as E1, without the confirmatory readings.

**E3. Simulation with a known truth (runs first).** Specification (robokit/simulation.py). The 10 Canadian reference funds; the true monthly covariance Sigma is the Ledoit-Wolf constant-correlation estimate over their extended history up to the first E1 estimation date (next paragraph). True means: world a, rf + 2.5 Sigma w_prior (the model is right); worlds b and c, the FP Canada 2026 planning return after each fund's fee, made arithmetic ((1 + g)^(1/12) - 1 + Sigma_ii / 2) (the model is wrong in a realistic way). Shocks: worlds a and b, multivariate Student t with 5 degrees of freedom and scale matrix Sigma x 3/5 (so the covariance is Sigma), independent months; world c, two regimes, calm and stress, staying with probability 0.97 and 0.85, stress covariance 2.5 times calm, scaled so the long-run covariance is Sigma, normal within a regime (clustered volatility: the case the stationary bootstrap is meant for). rf is the Canadian bill yield of the data snapshot, constant. Each method sees the simulated history through the engine's own data loader, so the product's code is what is tested. Common random numbers: one history per (world, replication), seen by every method.

The true covariance and the QIS study's truths use only months up to and including each market's first E1 estimation date (E1 tests only the months after it) (computed by the same rule as E1: December 2009 in Canada, December 2018 in the US), so the defaults E3 locks are not informed by any month E1 tests: Canada's truth uses the 61 months from December 2004 to December 2009; the QIS truths use exactly the 120 months January 2009 to December 2018, from 20 US funds with a return in every one of them. The planning returns of worlds b and c are 2026 published assumptions, not past returns.

Truth and loss. At level L the target is L x 20% of the true volatility of the policy-weighted equity slots; the true optimum maximises mu_excess'w - (gamma/2) w'Sigma w under the engine's limits at that level, gamma set so its true volatility equals the target. Primary measure: the certainty-equivalent loss, CE(true optimum) - CE(method's weights) at that gamma, in basis points a year (it charges both a return shortfall and a missed risk level at the level's own risk aversion). This optimum is continuous (no 2% minimum position), so each method's loss includes what its own discretisation costs; in the resampling rule's paired differences that term cancels. A second oracle applies the engine's minimum-position rule to the truth (drop positions under 2%, re-solve and recalibrate, round after round), and the loss against it is reported as well. Paired contrasts against robokit (mean difference, Monte Carlo standard error and number of pairs) are reported for every method. Secondary: true volatility minus target, the weight distance to the optimum, turnover between estimates 12 months apart (192 months simulated; estimates on months 1-180 and 13-192); for robokit, the share of all ten funds whose true optimal weight lies inside its 5-95% resampling band (a fund the final mix does not hold has the band [0, 0]) (the band is labelled as the spread of the resampled weights, not as a confidence interval, whatever this shows).

Size. 500 replications per world (the Monte Carlo standard error of each mean loss and of each paired difference is reported). A decision rule runs only on its full sample: if any of its 500 pairs is missing (a failed replication), the run stops and no default is locked. The run also stops unless the truths use the windows written here (Canada: the 61 months December 2004 to December 2009; QIS: January 2009 to December 2018). Level 3, every method (the E1 set: robokit, the four benchmarks, equal_weight, the seven ablations, textbook_60); levels 2 and 4, robokit and no_resample. Sub-study (i), the 36-month minimum: robokit in world b, level 3, with 36, 60, 120 and 180 months of data (the 180-month arm is the main grid's level-3 robokit row in world b, the same replications, not run twice; every replication simulates one 192-month path and a shorter study takes its first months, so the four window lengths see the same draws).

Rule for resampling (locks before E1). Per replication, d = loss(robokit) - loss(no_resample) at level 3 (positive: resampling costs certainty equivalent). Resampling stops being the default only if the one-sided 95% lower bound of the mean of d (Student t, 499 degrees of freedom) exceeds 2 basis points in BOTH world b and world c; world a is reported, not used (the product works in a world where the model is wrong).

Sub-study (ii), the QIS switch at N/T = 0.25 (locks before E1). Twenty funds drawn once (seed 20261008) from those of the 43-fund US list with a return in each of the 120 months January 2009 to December 2018, their covariance over exactly those months (the run refuses if the 120 months are not all there); two truths: their constant-correlation shrunk covariance (structured, favouring linear shrinkage) and their sample covariance (unstructured). For N/T = 0.10, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30, 0.40 and 0.60 (T = 200, 100, 80, 67, 50, 33 months), 500 normal samples; for each, the long-only minimum-variance portfolio (50% cap) from constant-correlation shrinkage and from QIS, and r = var_true(QIS) / var_true(constant correlation) - 1. Three primary one-sided tests per truth, Holm-adjusted within the truth: T1, QIS better at 0.20 (mean r below -1%); T2, QIS worse at 0.25 (mean r above +1%); T3, QIS better at 0.30 (mean r below -1%). Rule, over the two registered truths by name (the run stops if either is missing): the switch moves down to 0.20 only if T1 holds after Holm in both truths and T2 is not significant even before Holm (raw one-sided p of 0.05 or more) in either; it moves up to 0.30 only the other way round; otherwise, contradictory results included, it stays at 0.25. Judging contradiction on the raw p-values keeps Holm's step-down coupling from turning contradictory evidence into a move. T3 is reported beside them and does not enter the rule. The other ratios are descriptive.

**E4. Covariance estimators (descriptive).** Walk-forward on the E1 dates, both markets: (a) long-only minimum-variance portfolios (50% cap) of the non-cash reference funds from the sample covariance, Ledoit-Wolf identity, Ledoit-Wolf constant correlation and QIS, held 12 months; loss = squared deviation of each month's return from the portfolio's mean over the estimation window (past-only centring); Diebold-Mariano against constant correlation (Newey-West, 6 lags); (b) the shipped pipeline at level 3 with only the estimator changed: realised volatility and Diebold-Mariano on squared deviations from the mean the weights in force predicted from the funds' 180 months up to their estimation date (past-only centring, as in (a)); a path that stops is recorded, not analysed; the centre uses the last 180 months in which every held fund has a return, as (a); (c) a forecast check with fixed weights (the policy prior over the non-cash funds): each estimator's predicted variance against the realised variance of the next 12 months (squared error and QLIKE). No default changes.

**E5. Sensitivities (descriptive).** At the latest date, at level 3 (the projection precision at levels 1-5): block lengths 3, 12 and the Politis-White estimate (Politis and White 2004 with the Patton, Politis and White 2009 correction, on the policy portfolio's monthly returns, rounded to whole months); estimation windows 120 and all months (no cap); home bias 0, 20 and 40% of equity (Canada) and a 60/40 prior without real estate and gold; sleeve caps halved and doubled; minimum position 0, 1 and 5%; per-fund maximum 35% and 70%; delta 1 and 4 (with lambda calibrated, the two mixes' model volatility must agree with the delta 2.5 mix within twice the calibration tolerance (2 x 5e-5), recorded with the gap, and the weight changes are reported; Proposition 1 itself is tested exactly at fixed lambda / delta, in the optimizer and through the final pipeline, tests/test_v3_propositions.py, while lambda stays inside its absolute bounds); the yardstick replaced by the home stock fund alone; 100, 200 and 500 resampled histories, each with seeds 1-10 (the Monte Carlo spread of the weights); each coefficient of the shelf score at 0.5 and 1.5 times its value (the ESG coefficient on the ESG shelf, where it applies; the slots whose fund changes, and the fee change). Walk-forward at level 3 (as E1): block 3, block 12, the Politis-White block, window 120, all months, home bias 0/20/40 (Canada), the 60/40 prior, the home-fund yardstick: realised ratio and Sharpe. Projection precision (rule, numerical accuracy only, applied by the code): the Monte Carlo standard error of the one-year 5th percentile at each level over seeds 1-10 (standard deviation over seeds / sqrt 10), at 2,000 and at 5,000 paths; if it exceeds 0.25 percentage points (0.0025 in the decimal returns the projection reports) at 2,000 paths for any level in either market, the product's default rises to 5,000 paths (one number, recorded in `locked_defaults`; the standard errors by market stay under E5); a level still above it at 5,000 is reported as a remaining limitation. Projections play no part in E1-E4, so this rule cannot change what they test.

**E6. Rebalancing on an account.** The E1 robokit level-3 targets, in each market from the first month every reference fund has its own price and an E1 target exists (the account follows the target in force) to the data end, at month-end adjusted closes at their real price levels (adjusted closes before today are scaled down by later distributions, so early unit prices are somewhat lower than traded then; stated with the results); the targets must cover every scheduled December (otherwise E6 is not run for that market, and path_errors says why); accounts of 100,000 with no contributions, 100,000 with 500 a month, and 10,000 with 500 a month. Rules: `ideal` (fractional units, back to target every month: the walk-forward's assumption), `monthly` (the production rebalancer with no band: whole units, minimum order, residual cash), `bands` (the production rebalancer as shipped), `annual` (back to target each January; otherwise the bands are off and only new money trades, so no fund, not even one the targets no longer hold, is sold for drift); `bands` with minimum orders of 0 and 250 as well as 50. Reported: return and volatility (time-weighted, after each month's trading costs; the month-end at which the starting cash is invested is chained into the first invested month, so its cost stays in the return), tracking error and return gap against `ideal`, total cost, orders, skipped orders, average cash, average largest drift, turnover; and each reference fund's largest order placed by the shipped bands on a 1,000,000 account, as a share of its average daily traded value (today's).

**E7. Data and input rules.** (a) The data-integrity screen at ln 1.5, ln 2 and ln 3: the funds each flags, with month, return and peer median. As built, the peers' median and the five-fund minimum count every same-class fund with data that month, the fund itself included; the stricter reading (the fund left out, at least five others) is reported beside it at ln 2. (b) Crisis-replay stand-ins: for every eligible fund with its own data through an episode, the stand-in the replay's own rule would give it with itself left out (robokit/stress.py `standin_peers`: the median episode return of eligible funds of the same class, region and market with data through the episode, else of the same class; no minimum count) against its actual episode return (mean and median absolute error by episode, asset class and peer rule; funds with no peer counted). (c) Each reference fund's margin over each liquidity screen. (d) Every peak-to-trough fall deeper than 15% of each market's home stock fund since 2000, and the crisis replay that covers it. (e) Outside the script, after the run (the script does not produce these, and the paper reports them as a separate check): by a research lane with an independent second check, sources dated and archived: each reference fund's 5-year annualised total return from our data against the issuer's published figure (differences over 0.25 points a year listed); a sample of 30 satellite-only and 30 core-eligible funds labelled from their prospectus category (agreement with our classification); the top five candidates of every slot checked as broad, low-cost index funds.

**E8. Extended histories, held out.** (a) Each reference fund against each written stand-in other than itself, over at least 24 shared months (the stand-in rule's own overlap; the funds with no such stand-in are listed), the stand-in converted to the portfolio currency only when its written rule says so (as the engine uses it), over the months both have their own data: correlation, tracking error, volatility ratio, mean gap.
