When the model is right (world a), Robo-Kit is within {{E3.summary.a.3.180.robokit.loss_bp|f1}} basis points a year of the best the truth allows. When expected returns follow the planning assumptions instead (world b), it loses {{E3.summary.b.3.180.robokit.loss_bp|f1}}: less than five of the seven other methods, but {{derived.e3.b.static_mix_advantage|f1}} basis points a year more than the static mix (paired standard error {{E3.paired_contrasts.b.static_mix.loss_bp.se|f2}}). The loss comes from the wrong prior, not from estimation: Robo-Kit's weights sit {{E3.summary.b.3.180.robokit.distance|f2}} from the true optimum (half the sum of absolute weight differences), against {{E3.summary.a.3.180.robokit.distance|f2}} in world a. Estimating means from the data costs more: {{derived.e3.b.sample_mu|f1}} basis points with sample means, and {{derived.e3.b.textbook_60|f1}} for the textbook optimizer.

**Resampling stays, without evidence that it helps.** Its mean cost was {{E3.resampling_rule.b.mean|f3}} basis points in world b and {{E3.resampling_rule.c.mean|f3}} in world c, with one-sided lower bounds of {{E3.resampling_rule.b.lo95_one_sided|f3}} and {{E3.resampling_rule.c.lo95_one_sided|f3}}, far from the 2 basis points that would drop it, but it gained nothing either, even in the regime world it is meant for. With equilibrium returns $\pi = \delta\hat{\Sigma}w^0$, each bootstrap draw's optimum stays close to the prior whatever the draw's covariance, so averaging changes little. The resampling band contained the true optimal weight for {{E3.summary.a.3.180.robokit.band_coverage|pct0}} of the funds in world a and {{E3.summary.b.3.180.robokit.band_coverage|pct0}} in world b, which is why it is shown as a spread, not a confidence interval.

**Risk accuracy and the sub-studies.** In world b, Robo-Kit's true volatility differed from its target by {{E3.summary.b.3.180.robokit.abs_vol_error|pp2}} points on average. With 36, 60, 120 and 180 months of data its loss was {{E3.summary.b.3.36.robokit.loss_bp|f1}}, {{E3.summary.b.3.60.robokit.loss_bp|f1}}, {{E3.summary.b.3.120.robokit.loss_bp|f1}} and {{E3.summary.b.3.180.robokit.loss_bp|f1}} basis points, so the 36-month minimum stays a data-quality rule. At $N/T = 0.25$, QIS changed the true variance of the minimum-variance portfolio by {{E3.qis_switch.by_ratio.constant_correlation.0.25.mean_rel|+pct2}} and {{E3.qis_switch.by_ratio.sample.0.25.mean_rel|+pct2}} under the two truths, far inside the 1% margin, so the switch stays at 0.25; the estimators part only at ratios the engine rarely reaches ({{E3.qis_switch.by_ratio.constant_correlation.0.6.mean_rel|pct1}} and {{E3.qis_switch.by_ratio.sample.0.6.mean_rel|pct1}} at $N/T = 0.6$).

## 6.8 Data, replays and extended histories (E7, E8)

**Crisis replays.** For a fund without data through an episode, the peer-median stand-in missed the fund's own return by {{E7.crisis_standins.by_rule.class, region and market.median_ae|pp1}} points (median, {{E7.crisis_standins.by_rule.class, region and market.n|int}} fund-episodes). Every fall of more than 15% in either home stock fund since 2000 is covered by a replay except the 2000-2002 bear market, so a mix's behaviour in a long, slow decline is not shown to clients.

**Stand-ins and back-fill.** The written stand-ins tracked their funds with a median correlation of {{derived.e8.standin_median_corr|f2}}; the one weak pair, BNDX and its unhedged stand-in ({{derived.e8.bndx_bwx_corr|f2}}), is refused by the stand-in rule. The regression back-fill for young own picks recovered hidden histories with median correlations of {{derived.e8.CA.median_corr|f2}} (Canada) and {{derived.e8.US.median_corr|f2}} (US), but in the US it tracked no better than the home stock fund.

**Audits after the run.** Three registered checks ran outside the evaluation script (reports in docs/research). Against the issuers' published five-year returns, 14 of 17 comparable reference funds agreed within 0.05 points a year. The largest gap, VALT's 1.20 points, is mostly the difference between its market price and its net asset value; the cause of XIC's and XRE's (0.31 and 0.25 points below) is not established, and it understates their returns slightly but not their risk. Each slot's top candidates are broad, low-cost index funds, except that the Canadian global-bond slot holds a US-only fund. And 140 funds labelled blind from the issuers' pages showed the fund-swap gate to be too permissive: of 18 sampled stock funds the engine called broad, only one was, so a client could have swapped a sector or single-country fund into a broad slot. The gate now requires the slot's own selection rule and a like-for-like label; on a held-out sample, 29 of 30 narrow and 26 of 30 broad labels agree with the issuers.

## 6.9 Run record and changes after the run

The first registered run stopped three of E2's paths: the benchmark code looked up a cash fund that a point-in-time shelf did not yet have, and the fail-closed rule recorded the gap rather than stretching the previous weights over it. The bug was fixed and disclosed, the first run archived, and the evaluation run again (RESEARCH_LOG section 11). Every number of the second run equals the first's except the run's provenance, those three paths and what is computed from them, so the evaluation is deterministic; both runs locked the same defaults.

Two disclosed changes followed: the shipped constants were set to the locked defaults, and the swap gate was fixed (Section 6.8) without touching the evaluated path, since no slot's candidate list changes and every point-in-time pick of E2 is still accepted. The paper was examined in four rounds by the same kind of examiners as the plan (Section 5.5); the final round found no error of fact, and its required changes were made. One examiner reviewed the swap-gate change and found a defect, which was fixed, and testing the fix found two more.

**A second audit.** After the paper's examination, six AI finders searched the code by running it, one per area, and an independent verifier re-ran each finding and tried to refute it. Both were of the author's own model family (the others' usage limits had been reached), so this is weaker evidence of independence than the examinations, but every finding was reproduced. Of 53 findings, 52 were true. Most were in the product (requests that should have been refused or flagged, numerical edge cases, differences between the two engines, the rebalancer, fund labels) and were fixed with regression tests, except one kept as a limitation (Section 7); five concerned the evaluation code, including the three points of Section 5.2. Re-running the whole evaluation on the corrected code (output/v3_verify, compared value by value by scripts/v3_verify_rerun.py) changed only what these points predict, chiefly the textbook optimizer's rows; Robo-Kit's own results and every other benchmark's were unchanged to the last digit. Every finding and ruling is in docs/DECISIONS.md.

**A mislabelled gold fund.** A later review, of the team's optimizer report, found that VALT, the Canadian gold fund, is its issuer's CAD-hedged unit (the unhedged unit is VALT.B) but was labelled unhedged, because its name carries no hedging word. The engine therefore extended it back to 2004 with unhedged gold (CGL-C, then GLD converted to Canadian dollars), where the stand-in rule calls for gold in US dollars, unconverted, which also tracks VALT's own returns more closely (correlation {{derived.valt.e8_corr|f3}}, against {{derived.valt.e8_reg_corr.0|f3}} and {{derived.valt.e8_reg_corr.1|f3}} for the registered stand-ins). A search of the fund list against issuers' documents found 36 other hedging labels the name rule got wrong, none on either reference shelf; all were corrected in version 3.0.2. Unlike the swap-gate fix, this one touches the evaluated path: E1 and E8(a) in Canada use VALT's stand-in. E2 does not, but one of the corrected funds, HUG, was its only Canadian gold fund with 36 months of its own history from 2012 to 2022; HUG tracks gold futures, so the swap gate now refuses it and the point-in-time shelf leaves gold out in those years (the registered E2's level-3 mixes held it in 2012 to 2015; E2 was not re-run). Re-running E1 Canada with only VALT relabelled, on the universe the registered run used (output/v3_valt_check, scripts/v3_valt_check.py; not a registered run), leaves realised over target volatility at {{derived.valt.ratio.2|f2}}, {{derived.valt.ratio.3|f2}} and {{derived.valt.ratio.4|f2}} ("{{derived.valt.reading|raw}}") and lowers Robo-Kit's Sharpe ratios by {{derived.valt.sharpe_drop_min|f3}} to {{derived.valt.sharpe_drop_max|f3}}. One reading changes: against the static mix at level 4 the adjusted non-inferiority p becomes {{derived.valt.static_p.4|f3}}, so that comparison is "inconclusive"; at level 3 it stays "not worse by more than 0.10" (p = {{derived.valt.static_p.3|f3}}). The registered results are reported as registered. The simulation's truth (E3) also contains VALT's stand-in before 2010 but was not re-run; its two decisions sat far from their thresholds (Section 6.1).
