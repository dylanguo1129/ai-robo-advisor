# AI-first investing assistants and chat-based builders: market notes for Robo-Kit

Researched 28 Sep 2026. Scope: Magnifi, Public (Alpha, Generated Assets, Agents), Robinhood (Cortex, Strategies), Composer, Betterment, Wealthfront, Wealthsimple, Titan, Range, Origin, Fiscal.ai, Perplexity Finance, Morningstar (Mo and successor), Bloomberg, open-source GitHub robo-advisors.

## Method and caveats (read first)

- Quotes come from pages loaded with WebFetch. WebFetch returns text through a summarising model, so quotes from HTML pages are "as returned by the fetcher". Two PDFs were extracted directly with pypdf and are exact: the Robinhood Cortex Assistant Disclosure and Magnifi's Form ADV Part 2A (dated March 30, 2026).
- **Blocked or failed:** reddit.com and old.reddit.com (tool refuses the domain), so **no Reddit evidence**. Also blocked: Wealthfront support centre (403), Wealthsimple help centre (403), thecollegeinvestor.com (403), businesswire (403), bloomberg.com (403), perplexity.ai (403), cnbc.com (403), fastcompany (403), fiscal.ai (429). Pages that render with JavaScript (magnifi.com, finimize) came back empty.
- User sentiment therefore comes from the Apple App Store and a few review sites. The aitools.xyz "reviews" of Alpha look like an aggregator with unverifiable names, so treat them as low credibility.
- A search-engine summary said "Wealthfront 3.0" launched on July 29, 2026. The source article (wealthmanagement.com) is actually dated **March 31, 2016**. That is an example of the date hallucinations this research had to screen out.
- Pricing conflicts between sources are listed under each product. Where sources disagree, no single figure should be treated as settled.

## 1. Magnifi (TIFIN), AI investing assistant and SEC-registered robo-adviser

- URL: https://magnifi.com/ (renders with JavaScript, so it came back empty)
- **Registration and model** (Form ADV, exact): "Magnifi employs large language models to decipher user inputs, which may include information about a Client's existing investment portfolio, and generate search outputs" (https://files.adviserinfo.sec.gov/IAPD/Content/Common/crd_iapd_Brochure.aspx?BRCHR_VRSN_ID=1032174)
- **Chat** (ADV, exact): "Through a chat bot experience, Magnifi gives users access to conversational investing guidance, portfolio insights, market intelligence, and educational resources"
- ADV, exact: "The chat bot employs a proprietary, closed environment AI model."
- **Advice scope** (ADV, exact): "Security recommendations are made solely based on the search terms and questions presented by the Client." This means the advice is search-driven, not questionnaire-driven.
- **Pricing** (ADV, exact): "subscription fee of $14 per month or $99 per year". The App Store matches: "Premium at $8.25/month (annual) or $14/month" (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/magnifi-invest-with-ai/id1574358920). WallStreetZen gives an older price: "Annual: $132 ($11/month equivalent...)" (https://www.wallstreetzen.com/blog/magnifi-review/).
- **Features** (App Store): Portfolio Health Score, Hidden Overlap Analysis, Fee Drag Auditor, Real-Time Catalyst Tracking, linking several brokerages
- **Ratings:** App Store 3.9 out of 5 from 414 ratings
- **Complaints** (App Store reviews page https://apps.apple.com/us/app/magnifi-invest-with-ai/id1574358920?see-all=reviews):
  - "Ask this question about 10 different ways and never got close to a numerical response"
  - "If you type in a question, it only ACTUALLY puts in an automated question for you". The chat swaps the user's question for a canned one.
  - "this bot is as helpful as a basic customer support bot on a website"
  - "The system is very slow. Slow to scroll and slow to answer."
  - "if you are in the chat interface...it is super easy to accidentally tap the text box"
  - Billing: "It IMMEDIATELY charged my account"
- **Praise:** "Worth it just for the ability to link all your other investment accounts"
- **Weaknesses:** refuses or deflects numeric questions, swaps in canned questions, slow, trial and billing friction. The assistant is only as good as the search terms.
- **Lessons for Robo-Kit:** never swap the user's question for a canned one. When a number is asked, answer with the tool-computed number (the Robo-Kit validator helps here). Keep chat input separate from card taps so a stray tap does not open the keyboard. Fee Drag and Overlap are good ideas; Robo-Kit already shows fees in dollars and could add an overlap check between funds.

## 2. Public.com: Alpha, Generated Assets, Agents

- **Generated Assets (GenA)** (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/investors-can-turn-ideas-into-an-investable-index-with-publics-launch-of-generated-assets-302454862.html):
  - "With a single prompt, investors can describe an idea, which the Generated Assets AI platform immediately turns into an index"
  - "The index is backtested against historical market returns"
  - "Please check GenA's output before making any investment decisions"
- **Agents** (launched March 31, 2026; https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/public-becomes-the-first-brokerage-to-introduce-ai-agents-for-your-portfolio-302729050.html):
  - "Before taking action, the AI asks follow-up questions to help map out a complete Agent workflow."
  - "When the logic looks right, the investor activates the Agent, and it goes to work."
  - "They can pause, stop, or edit any agent workflow at any time."
  - "You are solely responsible for determining the suitability of any strategy and for verifying your instructions before activating your Agent."
- **App Store:** 4.7 out of 5 from 85K ratings (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/public-com-stocks-crypto/id1204112719). Description line: "Create an investing Agent from a prompt, or customize a ready-made Agent".
- **Review sentiment** (low credibility, aitools.xyz): "I still find myself verifying details through other platforms, which takes extra time."
- **Pattern:** prompt, then the AI asks follow-up questions, then a structured spec the user reviews, then one explicit Activate. The chat produces a single object and does not scatter state across turns.
- **Weaknesses:** GenA backtests of themed stock baskets invite hindsight bias (a single prompt "immediately" produces a backtested index). The user carries all suitability responsibility.

## Cross-cutting patterns (evidence above)

1. **The AI proposes and a deterministic UI confirms.** Examples: Robinhood's pre-filled ticket and "one order per conversation", Public Agents' follow-up questions then Activate, Composer's AI writing into an editable visual editor, Betterment's "advisor-built logic with AI-generated explanations". The chat never holds the state. A structured object does (ticket, agent spec, symphony, mix).
2. **Numbers come from deterministic engines, and the AI narrates.** Examples: Origin (deterministic modules plus 138 checks), open-source intikhab49 and AliJaffri, and Fiscal.ai (curated database plus citations). Robo-Kit's validator that rejects numbers missing from tool results fits this camp. Perplexity's factor-of-1,000 error shows the remaining gap: unit and scale.
3. **Provenance in the interface.** Examples: Bloomberg transparency links, Fiscal.ai per-answer citations (audit gated to the top tier), and the list of data sources in Robinhood's Digests methodology. Wealthsimple goes the other way and tells users not to rely on AI figures at all.
4. **Short questionnaires win.** Wealthfront uses "only a few questions" instead of the typical 25, Wealthsimple a "short suitability questionnaire", and Composer's Help Me Choose three questions (UNSOURCED). Robo-Kit's 1 + 15 steps is long by comparison.
5. **Risk override is allowed with friction.** Betterment gives users the final say, a "too aggressive" flag and named bands. Wealthfront says users can change the score but should not change it often. Wealthsimple (UNSOURCED) reportedly caps by financial situation but gives a reassessment path. No verified product silently disables a level with no path forward.
6. **Standalone chatbots are giving way to assistants built into workflows.** Morningstar retired Mo, and Robinhood's Assistant has scoped actions and limited memory.
7. **Recurring user complaints:**
   - AI will not give numbers or answers the wrong question (Magnifi)
   - slowness (Magnifi)
   - hidden subscription costs (Composer, Magnifi, Titan)
   - no human support (Composer)
   - data accuracy that users must re-verify (Alpha)
   - backtest caveats buried (Composer)
8. **Disclosure language is converging:** not advice, may contain errors or hallucinations, hypothetical backtests, the user verifies before acting. Robinhood adds "not intended to predict or project performance".
